Charge is a physical property of an object like math

Protons -> positively charged
Electrons -> negatively charged

atom -> # protons = # electrons

atoms naturally electrically uncharged, unless they are charged

because nucleus cant be broken, an atom can only be charged into an Ion by adding or removing electrons

unit for charge on an object: q or Q

SI unit for charge: Coulomb: C

point charge, has no dimension (weight/thickness)

an electric field is the effect of other objects from a charge

point charge is a source of an electric field, and each field is unique to each objects' property

its very important to only couple interactions between fields and their related properties, not properties that arent related to that field. for example you cant interact mass and electrical fields, or charge and gravitational  fields, you must interact charge with electrical fields 

when an object has a charge it will have an electric field, which gets stronger with the distance from and strength of a charge

an e-field is due to a positive or negative charge

when drawing a field the arrow should never start at the charge, it should point in the direction of the field, for example the arrow points away from a positively charged point and towards a negatively charged point 

The magnitude of due E-field is due to point charges or spherically symmetric charges but outside of the charge
$$
|\vec{E}| = \frac{k|q|}{r^{2} }(\pm \hat{r})
$$
k is a constant of electrostatic
$$
k=9\times10^{9} \frac{Nm^{2}}{c} (\text{in a vacuum or air})
$$
$$
k=\frac{1}{4\pi \epsilon_{0}}(\text{in a vacuum})
$$
$\epsilon_{0}:$ permittivity of air/vacuum
$r$: distance from point charge or senter of the spherical symmetric charge
$\hat{r}$: unit vector along r
	$+\hat{r}$: pointing away from source charge
	$-\hat{r}$ pointing toward source charge

## How to Calculate E-fields due to various types of sources. 
it is NOT $\frac{kq}{r^{2}}$
One method is Gauss' law

## Gauss' Law:
If we fully enclose a source of a charge(Q) or mass(m) with some (any) enclosing surface, then the total Flux of an E-Field due to Q (or g-field due to m) is proportional to the charge enclosed by that surface (or mass)

### What is Flux of some stuff?
Flux of any stuff is the total amount of that stuff over some surface.
all forms of Flux are scalars!!

### How to calculate Flux of any vector quantity $\vec{V}$
$\phi$ - Symbol for Flux

$\phi _\vec{V}=\int \vec{V}_{/ / da}\vec{} \times d\vec{a}$
where $\vec{V}_{ / / d\vec{a}}$ means the component of the vector $\vec{V}$ at a point parallel to a portion of the area ($d\vec{a}$ ) at that point

because of this we can re-write as a dot product
$\phi_{\vec{V}}=\int \vec{V} \cdot d\vec{a}$
$\phi_{\vec{E}}=\int \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{a}$

if $\vec{E}_{ / / d\vec{a}}$ (or $\vec{V}_{ / / d\vec{a}}$) is uniform over a surface we can drop the integral
then $\phi_{\vec{E}}=\vec{E}_{/ / d\vec{a}}*\vec{A}$
